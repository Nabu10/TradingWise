The idea

Identical logic to the stock version, just priced per contract instead of per share. Once the premium is up by your target gain, close only enough contracts to get back everything you paid — the rest are riding at zero net cost.

Why ×100

Option premiums are quoted per share, but one contract controls 100 shares. A $2.50 premium costs $250 per contract — that's the real dollar amount at risk, and what this calculator works from.

Worth knowing

This is a position-sizing technique, not investment advice. It doesn't account for time decay, assignment risk, or bid/ask spread — check your own numbers and the contract's actual liquidity before trading.
